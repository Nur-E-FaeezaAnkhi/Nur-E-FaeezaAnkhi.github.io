Project 1 · 2023 – present (from my undergraduate thesis)

Extreme weather and urban travel

How heat, heavy rain and waterlogging change the way Dhaka travels.

Natural Hazards · 2025

Investigating sensitivity to extreme weather of a mid-size global south city

Research question
Which parts of Dhaka are most sensitive to extreme heat and waterlogging together?
Method
GIS-based multi-criteria analysis (AHP) with spectral indices and physical, hydrological and geomorphological variables.
Key findings
  • Only about 20% of the city has low sensitivity.
  • Almost half (49%) is highly or very highly susceptible.
Transportation · 2026

Navigating Dhaka's extreme weather: inconveniences and modal shifts among commuters and non-commuters

Research question
Who switches travel mode during extreme weather, and why?
Method
Survey of 318 households in 12 high- and low-sensitivity zones; binary logistic regression.
Key findings
  • Women, older adults and high-income travellers are the most likely to switch modes.
  • Men, commuters and vehicle owners tend to stay with their usual mode.
  • Waterlogging, checking the weather and having an alternative route all make a switch more likely.
Presented in person · TRB 105th Annual Meeting · 2026

Towards Resilient Transportation for Dhaka: Analyzing the Dynamics of Travel Adaptation Strategies in Response to Extreme Weather

Research question
How do commuters and non-commuters adapt their travel to extreme heat, heavy rain and waterlogging?
Method
318 respondents in 12 high- and low-sensitivity zones; binomial and multinomial logistic regression. I presented this poster in Washington, D.C.
Key findings
  • Adaptation strategies differ by weather type and by location.
  • They also differ across socio-economic and demographic groups.
Under review · Travel Behaviour and Society

Weather variability and spatial heterogeneity in urban travel: Evidence from statistical, spatial, and machine learning analyses

Research question
Do weather and location change mode choice, travel time and cost, and which matters more?
Method
1,984 trips; multinomial and multilevel logit, Moran's I, Random Forest, XGBoost and SHAP.
Key findings
  • Weather strongly changes mode choice and raises travel time, waiting time and cost.
  • Location matters too, but weather is the dominant driver.
Under review · Transportation Research Part D

Traveling through extreme weather: A statistical and machine learning approach

Research question
Who cancels, reschedules, reroutes or works from home when extreme weather hits?
Method
1,778 observations; binary and multinomial logit, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost and SHAP.
Key findings
  • Trip purpose and income are the strongest determinants.
  • Waterlogging triggers more adaptation than extreme heat.
  • Non-commuters adapt far more than commuters.
Project 2 · 2025 – present

Congestion, land use and traffic operations

Why Dhaka's roads jam, from the whole network down to one intersection.

Under review · Journal of Transport Geography

Thirteen hours of slowdown: When, where and around what land uses Dhaka's roads congest

Research question
When and where does Dhaka congest, and around which land uses?
Method
699,840 ten-minute readings on 81 road segments (April to June 2026); panel event-study models, spatial statistics, spatial regression and gradient boosting with SHAP.
Key findings
  • No familiar twin peaks: the slowdown stretches across one long block of the day.
  • Some parts of the city congest mainly at night.
  • A major holiday cut congestion sharply on the same roads, so demand, not road supply, drives it.
Under review · PLOS ONE

Case study: Performance of a heterogeneous urban T-intersection under different scenarios

Research question
How do small changes in driver behaviour affect a mixed-traffic intersection?
Method
PTV VISSIM microsimulation with the Wiedemann 74 car-following model across 12 behaviour scenarios.
Key findings
  • Small changes in following distance, lane discipline and attentiveness ripple through the whole intersection.
  • They shift capacity, delay, travel time, fuel use and emissions.
Submitted

Traffic Conditions Around an Urban School Under Intensifying Surrounding Land Use: A Case Study of Dhaka

Research question
How does a large urban school affect the roads around it, and what would fix it?
Method
Counts at five intersections and three pedestrian locations; focus groups and interviews with the school, residents and traffic police.
Key findings
  • Peak hour factor above 0.90 at four of five intersections.
  • School-side roads jam because of missing off-street parking and encroached footpaths; arterials jam from unmanaged through traffic.
  • Proposes a managed school transport service and other short- and long-term measures.

In preparation: Where do jams start? Congestion propagation across Dhaka's road network and Jams start with demand but end alike: Onset and duration of congestion in Dhaka.

Project 4 · 2025 – present

Active transportation

What makes walking and cycling safe and pleasant.

TRB 106th Annual Meeting · 2027

Listening to the Streets: Perception of Non-Motorized Transport Users in Cox's Bazar Municipality

Research question
How satisfied are walking and cycling users with services, and where are the gaps?
Method
Structured survey; chi-square tests, correlation, principal component analysis and hierarchical clustering.
Key findings
  • Satisfaction rests on four dimensions: comfort, urban services, safety, and parking and pedestrian facilities.
  • The biggest gaps are in parking, safety, sidewalk quality and the environment.

Project 5 · Regional planning

Regional development and equity

Who gets services, growth and development across Bangladesh.

ICIURP 2027 · Accepted

Spatial Variation in Human Development Index (HDI) across Bangladesh and Its Underlying Causes

Research question
How does human development vary across districts, and why?
Method
District HDI from national statistics, four classification methods and Moran's I.
Key findings
  • All highly developed districts are in Dhaka and Chattogram divisions.
  • Lagging Rangpur and Mymensingh received about 3% of the budget each, against 39% for Dhaka.
ICIURP 2027 · Accepted

Does Functional Region Diverge from Administrative Boundary? District Catchments in Barishal Division

Research question
Why do districts' real catchments differ from their official boundaries?
Method
Reilly's law of retail gravitation in GIS, then a comparison of contrasting districts.
Key findings
  • Patuakhali's reach is 6% larger than its boundary; Jhalokathi's is about 60% smaller.
  • Population change, job diversity and connectivity matter more than density.
Under review · PLOS ONE

Understanding the dynamics of rural livelihood strategy choices in Bangladesh

Research question
Why do rural households diversify, intensify or migrate?
Method
452 household surveys, 12 focus groups and 23 interviews in 10 villages; ordinal and multinomial logit.
Key findings
  • Household size, income, land ownership, shocks and electricity access shape choices.
  • Roads have improved in most villages, but other amenities lag behind.
Submitted · Social Indicators Research

Does every corner of a developing country receive equitable public facility provision?

Research question
How evenly are schools, health facilities and markets spread across 64 districts?
Method
Location quotients, Local Moran's I clusters and a threshold-population gap analysis.
Key findings
  • Health is the most unequal: 30% of provision serves 50% of people.
  • 22% of districts are severely deprived.
  • The strongest deprivation cluster sits at Dhaka's own fringe, not in remote areas.
Submitted · Social Indicators Research

Analysis of economic growth and regional competitiveness: A long-run approach in a coastal delta

Research question
Which industries drive growth and competitiveness in Barishal's districts?
Method
Shift-share analysis of 2003 and 2013 employment, Klassen typology and net-shift regionalisation.
Key findings
  • Wood products, non-metallic minerals and wholesale grew fastest; retail, hotels and education slowest.
Project 6 · Urban planning

Informal economy, housing and land use

Planning for the urban poor and for land under pressure.

Cities · 2025

Formal management structure of informal market: Bou Bazar of Dhaka

Research question
How does an informal market under formal management actually work?
Method
Focus groups, interviews and participatory rapid appraisal tools.
Key findings
  • Limited operating hours make efficient use of public space.
  • Vendors are more satisfied when autonomy is balanced with outside management.
  • The service fee served no clear purpose beyond exploitation.
City and Environment Interactions · 2026

Ensemble tree-based ML for agro-industrial land suitability: Narayanganj City

Research question
Where can agro-industry grow without losing farmland and water?
Method
Random Forest and XGBoost in GIS, with SHAP, on nine spatial variables (about 94% accuracy).
Key findings
  • 38% of the city is suitable, but only 13% highly suitable.
  • Only 8% of suitable land overlaps the industrial zones in the Detailed Area Plan.
Under review · PLOS ONE

Transforming Dhaka's slums: Suitable locations and community-preferred upgradation strategies

Research question
Which slums suit upgrading, and which approach do residents prefer?
Method
Multi-criteria decision-making over 29 DAP priority sites, then focus groups at the selected sites.
Key findings
  • 12 of the 29 sites are suitable.
  • About half prefer on-site upgrading and a quarter prefer sites-and-services.
  • Owners and tenants want different things.
